## Context

My thesis was a clinical inventory system covering medicines, supplies, orders, quotes, patients and warehouses. The CRUD wasn’t the interesting part. Two other things were: **fine-grained permissions** and **free text**.

## Architecture

- **NestJS backend** with modules for inventory, medicines, medical supplies, orders, patients, quotes, warehouses, categories and roles/permissions, on PostgreSQL and Docker Compose.
- **Python AI microservice (FastAPI + Ollama):** takes a free-text note (an illustrative example: *“out of size M gloves, order for two weeks”*) and returns structured restocking parameters. The model is `qwen2.5:7b-instruct`, running locally.
- **Next.js 15 / React 19 frontend** with per-warehouse roles and permissions in middleware and navigation, transfers, history and user management.

## Why local AI

In a clinical setting, sending text to an external API is not a decision to make by default. For extracting parameters from a short note, a local 7B model was enough, and the clinic’s information stays on its own server.

## What I learned

AI works better as a **translator into structure** than as a decision-maker. The model proposes parameters and the system, with its rules and permissions, decides.
